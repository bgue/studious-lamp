# WebXR (experimental)

Look at the model in virtual reality (a headset), place it in augmented reality (a headset with passthrough, or a phone), or lay it at real size over the real building on site to see the services behind walls and ceilings (**field AR**, see below). Tool `xr` (label `WebXR`) in the action panel, folder `WebXR` in the panel, actions `xr.vr`, `xr.ar` and `xr.field` (no default keys). Everything is feature detected: the viewer asks `navigator.xr.isSessionSupported('immersive-vr')` and `('immersive-ar')`; when neither is supported (and AR Quick Look is not there either, see "iPhone and iPad") the tool, the folder and the buttons stay hidden, and the viewer behaves exactly as before. **Experimental**: it was developed and checked against the Immersive Web Emulation Runtime (iwer), not on a headset (see "What was verified" below).

| Control (folder `WebXR`) | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `Enter VR` | Start an `immersive-vr` session at real scale. Hidden when VR is not supported. Call it from a click or a key press (a browser rule). |
| `Enter AR` | Start an `immersive-ar` session (hit-test feature requested as optional). Hidden when AR is not supported. |
| `Enter field AR` | Start an `immersive-ar` session at real scale for the building site (features `hit-test`, `anchors`, `dom-overlay` requested as optional). Hidden when AR is not supported. |
| `AR scale` | `1:1` (real size) or `tabletop 1:50` (the model looks 50 times smaller). Changing it during a session re-applies the placement at once. |
| `field look` | The look of a field session: `x-ray` (services only), `ghosted` (default) or `full`. |
| `XR status` | Read-only text: what the session is doing and which passes it leaves out. |
| `pointer rays` | Draw the ray of each controller or hand (blue; yellow when it meets an element). |

The drawer of the tool has the same buttons plus `Leave XR session` and a longer status line, the section **Field AR on site** (below) and the section **iPhone and iPad** (`Open in AR Quick Look` or `Download USDZ`, `Share link as QR`). Params section `xr` is `{ scale, pointerRays, fieldView, cutRadius, storeyCut }` (not in the share URL). `window.__xrayXr = { enter(mode), exit(), state(), setArScale(s), config, manager, field, usdz, qr() }`; `enter('field')` starts field AR, `state().field` is its state.

## VR

- Real scale: one model metre is one metre in the room. The session uses the `local` reference space, so the head starts at the origin of that space and the viewer puts it exactly on the **current orbit camera position**, looking along the horizontal part of the orbit view direction. (Head height above the floor is whatever the headset reports; the viewer does not add an eye height.)
- Controllers or hands draw a pointer ray (`pointer rays`). **Trigger or pinch** (`select`) selects the element under the ray (`app.selectIds`, the same BVH raycast and the same rules as the mouse: hidden and click-through ghosts are skipped, cut parts do not count); selecting nothing clears the selection. **Squeeze** toggles X-ray. The first pointer also drives the hover highlight (`app.hoverId`).
- **Right thumbstick**: snap turn by 30 degrees (push past 0.7, back below 0.3 to re-arm). The turn pivots about the head, so the head does not move in the world. **Left thumbstick**: walk in the horizontal plane along the head direction (2 m/s). There is no teleport.

## AR

- Before the first tap the model stands 1.2 m ahead of you (1:1, floor 1.4 m below the eyes) or 0.9 m ahead on a table height (tabletop). With the `hit-test` feature a white ring follows the surface under the view centre; **select** (tap, trigger, pinch) places the model so that the centre of its footprint (at its lowest point) lands on the ring. Without hit-test the select places it where it stands. Afterwards a select picks an element like in VR. **Squeeze** picks the model up again (the next select places it); in AR the thumbsticks do not move you.
- The canvas clears to alpha 0 and the scene background is removed, so the camera image shows through; both are restored on exit.
- `tabletop 1:50`: the viewer is made 50 times larger than the model (the rig scale, see below), so a 25 m building is 0.5 m long on the table. The pointer ray, the controllers and the head keep their physical size; the model is not rescaled.

## Field AR (on site)

Lay the model at real size over the real building to see the ducts, pipes and cables behind walls and ceilings. It is a separate mode from the tabletop AR above: the model is never scaled, and it is placed by **site registration**, not by a single tap. Code: `src/xr/fieldSession.ts` (session logic), `src/xr/field.ts` (pure maths and the saved format), `src/xr/fieldOverlay.ts` (phone panel), `src/xr/hud.ts` (text panel for headsets).

### Where it works

Honest state: developed and checked against the iwer emulator only (see "What was verified"); nothing below was tried on a real device.

Light estimation and depth sensing (occlusion of the model by real objects) are not used: the x-ray materials are flat, and the real walls already hide nothing because the model is drawn over the camera image. `local` is the reference space (gravity aligned on ARCore and Quest).

### Registration by point pairs

1. **Model points.** Before the session, in the normal view: drawer button `Pick model points`, then click wall corners, column centres or floor marks on the model (at least 2, far apart on plan; magenta dots; click the button again to stop). A pick ignores ghosting (a nearly invisible wall still counts) and snaps in plan to the nearest vertex of the element within `snapRadius` (10 cm), keeping the picked height, so a click near a wall corner lands on its vertical edge. In the session the same can be done by ray: in step 1 the model shows in full (the walls must be visible to aim at them) and a select (tap, trigger, pinch) on the model adds a point. A floating mini-model was not built.
2. **Room points.** In the session, aim at the same real features in the same order (the pink marker shows which one) and select. On a phone the white dot is the hit test of the **screen centre** (crosshair; tap the camera image or `Mark room point`); on a headset it is the hit test of **the controller ray** that selects (a hit-test source per controller). The point is the hit-test result in the reference space.
3. **Solve.** When every model point has its room point, the pairs are solved by the point cloud solver (`solvePairs` of `src/pointcloud/register.ts`, keep vertical: a 2D Kabsch fit of the yaw on plan plus the mean vertical offset, real scale). The device frame is gravity aligned, so the model is never tilted; a full 3D rotation from picking noise would tilt it, which is why it is not offered. The result is the rig of the session (`world = rigPosition + Ry(yaw) * local`). The panel shows each residual (plan and height parts, and the RMS); with more than 2 pairs a pair more than 10 cm off is named.
4. **Floor.** `Floor here` (phone) or `Y` (left controller) puts the floor of the storey you stand on at the hit point: only the height changes. Any horizontal hit 0.4 to 2.5 m below the eyes also counts as the floor for the eye height (else 1.5 m is assumed, and the elevation line says so).
5. **Nudge.** Phone: the `Nudge` pad moves the model left, right, away, closer, up, down by 1 cm (or 10 cm, `step` button) relative to where you look, and turns it by 0.5 degrees (or 2) about the centroid of the room points. Headset: flick the left stick for the plan moves, the right stick sideways to turn and up or down for the height, one step per flick.
6. **Anchor.** After the solve the session creates a WebXR anchor at the centroid of the room points (`XRFrame.createAnchor`). Every frame the rig follows the anchor's pose (`followAnchor`): when the device corrects its map while you walk, the model moves with the room. Without the `anchors` feature (or a request that does not settle in 4 s) the status says `no anchors: may drift while you walk`; register again then (`Register again` keeps the model points and asks for the room points).
7. **Save and restore.** `Save site` stores the pairs, the rig, the residuals and the anchor pose in `localStorage` (`xray.xr.sites.<model>`, schema `xray-xr-site/1`, newest 20, keyed by a fingerprint of the model); where the browser offers persistent anchors (Quest) the anchor's handle is stored too. With `restore saved site` on (default), the next field session restores the newest site: with a handle it asks for the anchor (`restorePersistentAnchor`) and puts the model back without a tap; otherwise it asks for the saved points again (2 or more taps, no model picking). `Export site` and `Import site` move a site between devices as JSON (an import of another model is refused).

### What the field view shows

- **Looks** (`field look`, panel buttons, squeeze on a headset): `ghosted` (default) keeps the services (the focus classes of the classification settings) as they are, caps walls, slabs and the other structure classes at 0.06 opacity and everything else at 0.2: the real walls are what you see, the model adds what is behind them. `x-ray` hides every class but the services. `full` is your own look. The looks are class styles laid over your styles for the session (`fieldClassStyles`); exit puts your styles back (the same object). A model without service classes (fzk-haus) shows nothing in `x-ray`, and its ghosted walls are click-through for a tap.
- **Cut around you.** A section box (the section plugin's box, caps and handles off) cuts away the model more than `cut radius (m)` (default 6, `Cut -` / `Cut +`) from your head on plan, and with `storey cut` at the ceiling of the storey you stand on (the floor of the next storey) and 1 m below its floor, so services under the floor stay. The storey comes from your height: feet = head height minus the eye height, then the highest storey floor at or below the feet plus 0.5 m (`storeyAt`). Storey floors are the IFC elevations moved by the vertical origin shift, or the median element bottom when the two disagree by more than 1.5 m. The box follows you (each 25 cm, or when the storey changes); `Cut` switches it off; exit restores your section settings.
- **Elevation line**: storey name, your eye height above its floor, its ceiling, and the height of the white dot; the card of a tapped element adds its bottom and top above the storey floor.

### Interaction

- **Tap an element** (screen tap through the DOM overlay on a phone, trigger or pinch on a headset): the same pick as the mouse (ghosts below 0.3 are passed, so a pipe behind a ghosted wall is picked). The card shows the name, class, storey, material and up to 4 properties of the hover card (`src/ui/hoverCard.ts`, the property filter of Hover info) and the **clearance**: the distance from the device to the closest point of the element (live, every 0.4 s) and from the white dot (the last hit-test point) to it.
- **Snapshot** (`Snapshot`, `A` on the right controller): a web page cannot read the camera image in WebXR, so the snapshot is the **rendered overlay only** (one view from the first XR view's pose and projection, into a private target, read back, PNG with a transparent background) plus the head pose, the element, the clearance and the storey. At the end of the session each snapshot becomes a **BCF topic** through `window.__xrayBcf.createTopic` when the BCF plugin is there and `snapshots to BCF` is on: title `Field note N`, label `field AR`, camera at the head pose, the topic snapshot rendered by the BCF plugin from that pose (model only), pin at the element, and a description that says the camera image is not available. `Download notes` saves all snapshots of the last session with their PNGs and poses as `xray-xr-notes/1` JSON; without the BCF plugin that is the only place they go.
- **Headset buttons**: trigger or pinch = select, squeeze = next look (or "done picking" in step 1), right `A` = snapshot, right `B` = register again, left `X` = cut on or off, left `Y` = floor here, sticks = nudges after the registration. A text panel floats below the line of sight with the status, the step, the residuals, the elevation and the card.

### Phone panel

The DOM overlay root (`#xray-xr-field`) covers the screen, lets taps through to the session and stops a tap on a button from also being a select (`beforexrselect`). Buttons are at least 48 px, the boxes keep clear of notches (`env(safe-area-inset-*)`), in portrait the panel sits at the bottom with the status and the card on top, in landscape it moves to the right edge. Buttons: `Exit`, `Mark point` (`Pick model point` in step 1, `Mark room point` in step 2), `Undo`, `Done picking`, `+ Pair`, `Register again`, `Floor here`, `Save site`, `X-ray` / `Ghosted` / `Full`, `Cut`, `Cut -`, `Cut +`, the `Nudge` pad with its step, `Measure`, `Snapshot`, `Clear`.

## iPhone and iPad

Safari on iPhone and iPad has no WebXR, so neither the tabletop AR nor the field AR runs there, in any browser (they all use WebKit). The fallback is **AR Quick Look**: `Open in AR Quick Look` (shown where `<a rel="ar">` is supported; elsewhere the same button is `Download USDZ`) exports what the viewer shows to USDZ with three's `USDZExporter` (loaded on demand) and opens it through a `rel="ar"` link. Each visible element keeps its current colour and its current x-ray alpha as the material opacity (elements of the same colour and alpha, quantised to 16 levels and 0.1 steps, share a mesh), hidden and invisible elements are left out, and the model is moved so that the centre of its footprint is at the origin and its lowest point at 0, with plane anchoring. **Quick Look places the model on a surface you tap, at real size, with no site registration and no interaction with the x-ray**: it is for showing, not for checking on site. Where Quick Look is missing the link downloads the file (the page is never navigated away). `Share link as QR` shows a QR code of the share link (`window.__xrayParams.shareUrl()`, or the model only when that is too long) to open the same view on a phone or a headset; the encoder is `src/export/qr.ts` (byte mode, levels L and M, versions 1 to 40, no dependency). A model opened from a local file is not on the server, so the other device needs the file too.

## The rig

The model never moves. The XR camera sits in a rig (an `Object3D` in the scene that also holds the pointer rays and the reticle): `world = rigPosition + Ry(yaw) * (scale * local)`, `local` being a position in the XR reference space. Walking and turning change position and yaw; the AR presets change `scale` (world metres per physical metre). Placing the model in AR is solving that equation for the rig position so that a world anchor lands on the hit point. The maths is `src/xr/maths.ts` (pure, tested); the session logic is `src/xr/session.ts`; `plugins/xr.ts` owns the GUI. The camera clip distances are physical metres (near 0.05, far at least 50).

## What a session draws

Inside a session `renderer.render` draws with three.js' stereo camera straight into the XR framebuffer. A render into a private target (OIT accumulation, the peel flood, imaging, post effects) would be drawn once per eye with the eye viewports, which is wrong for a single-view target, so these passes are left out, and the status line says which:

| Feature | In a session |
| --- | --- |
| Normal passes, edges, sorted ghost pass, focus pass | Drawn per eye (the shaders read `viewMatrix`, `projectionMatrix` and `cameraPosition` per view). |
| X-ray (algorithms 1 and 2) | Runs from the **XR camera's world position** (the head), throttled as before: `SIGHTLINE_INTERVAL_MS`, and only after the head moved by 1 cm (in rig scale). |
| Behind-only | Works; the plane is perpendicular to the **head's view direction** through the nearest focus point. |
| Sections (box, plane) and their caps | Work (a per-fragment test in world coordinates). |
| Order-independent ghosts | **Off**, the sorted ghost path draws. Tested with the emulator: with a forced OIT the right eye is wrong even where both eyes share one framebuffer, and on real devices the eyes may sit in separate textures or array layers. `renderer.xrForceOit` exists only for that test. |
| Peelback window | **Off** (mask and jump flood are single-view screen-space targets; picking ignores it too). |
| Imaging (radiograph, MRI) | **Off**, the standard renderer draws; status says `imaging radiograph drawn as standard`. Imaging renders into screen-sized float targets, which neither fit the XR framebuffer layouts nor the per-eye views. |
| Post pipeline (AO, bloom, depth of field, vignette) | **Off**. |

Exit restores everything: the renderer is the normal one (`renderer.xr.enabled` is false again, not just not presenting), the camera is detached from the rig and gets its orbit position, orientation, scale, clip distances and field of view back, the orbit controls are enabled, the background and the clear alpha come back, the canvas is resized to the page, and Algorithm 2 runs from the orbit camera again. The frame loop is the same one before, inside and after the session.

## Frame loop

`app.start()` hands the frame function to `renderer.renderer.setAnimationLoop`: three.js uses `window.requestAnimationFrame` outside a session and the session's own `requestAnimationFrame` inside one. Frame timing statistics and every plugin hook (`onFrame`, `afterFrame`) run in both. A new hook `beforeFrame(app, now, dt)` runs first in every frame; the XR plugin uses it to read the head pose and the controllers, move the rig and bring the camera up to date for the hooks that follow (view-mode planes, sections). `runSightlinesNow` uses `renderer.cameraWorldPosition()`.

## What was verified, and how

- Pure maths (`tests/xr-maths.test.ts`): rig transform (also against a three.js `Object3D`), pose to camera, orbit camera to head, controller ray to world ray, snap turn and its re-arming, walking, the AR placement and both scale presets. Feature detection with fake `navigator.xr` objects (`tests/xr-support.test.ts`).
- **Headless with iwer** (`bench/smoke-xr.mjs`; `iwer` 2.7.0 is a dev dependency, MIT licence, with its own dev-only dependencies `gl-matrix` (MIT) and `webxr-layers-polyfill` (Apache-2.0), injected into the page by the script with `page.addInitScript`, never part of the app or of the bundles: the `npm run build` and `npm run bundle` outputs contain no iwer code, `src/ui/params.ts` imports only `version` from `package.json` so even the name does not appear, and `tests/xr-iwer.test.ts` checks that no file under `src/` mentions it). The emulated Meta Quest 3 gets installed as `navigator.xr`; the script enters VR from the GUI button, checks the camera starts on the orbit position, aims the emulated right controller at the element under the screen centre, pulls the trigger and asserts the selection, squeezes (X-ray toggles), pushes the thumbstick (a 30 degree snap turn, head fixed), walks with the left stick (sightlines run again), turns imaging, peel, behind-only and a section on (the fall back list), exits and asserts that the normal renderer is restored (xr off, no rig, camera scale 1, orbit controls on, frame loop running, the picture pixel identical to the one before entering), then enters AR, places the model with a select and switches to `tabletop 1:50`.
- **Field AR, headless with iwer** (`bench/smoke-xr-field.mjs`). The emulated Quest 3 gets a stub synthetic environment (a class given to `device.installSEM` in the script, not the `@iwer/sem` package): the hit test of every ray is its crossing with one horizontal plane whose height the script sets per target, so the room points are known exactly in iwer's global frame. A true site transform (yaw 0.6 rad plus a shift) maps that frame onto fzk-haus. The script picks a model point with a click in the normal view, enters field AR from the panel button, checks the phone panel (buttons present, at least 44 px) and the in-scene text panel (iwer has no `dom-overlay`), taps 3 room points with the emulated controller (2 on the ground floor, 1 on the upper floor), and compares what the app sees at 2 check points (2 m and 9 m away) with the true transform: **exact taps give residuals and check-point errors below 0.001 mm; taps off by up to 1 cm (a fixed pattern) give residuals 0.61, 0.78, 0.80 cm (RMS 0.73 cm) and check-point errors 0.45 and 0.81 cm**. Then: the anchor is tracked; the 3 looks set the class styles; the cut box has the storey floors as its bottom and top and follows when the head climbs a storey; a trigger on a wall gives the card and the clearance; a nudge moves the model 1 cm and turns it 0.5 degrees; `Save site` stores a persistent anchor handle; exit restores the renderer, the class styles object and the section settings and the picture is pixel identical; a second session restores the site from the persistent anchor without a tap (check points within 3 cm, the accuracy of the noisy registration), a snapshot keeps an overlay PNG and becomes a BCF topic at exit; a third session without the `anchors` feature falls back to re-tapping the saved points; the USDZ export is a zip with every visible element, and the `rel="ar"` link downloads it in Chromium without leaving the page. Unit tests: `tests/xr-field.test.ts` (registration, noise, nudges, anchor following, snapping, storeys, cut box, looks, saved format and storage), `tests/xr-usdz.test.ts` (buckets, centring, zip layout with `model.usda` first and 64-byte aligned, usda contents), `tests/xr-qr.test.ts` (a symbol equal bit for bit to segno's, Reed-Solomon, capacities, format bits). The emulator cannot show real tracking drift, real plane detection, a real phone panel in `dom-overlay`, or what 1 cm of tap error looks like on a real corner: on a phone expect several centimetres from the hit test itself.
- **Not verifiable here**: anything that needs a real device. The layout of the XR framebuffer (side by side, per-view textures, texture arrays or multiview) and with it the verdict on OIT and imaging on real hardware; real hit-test and the reticle (the emulator has no environment, so the script covers the placement without a surface); hand tracking (the same `select` event, not exercised); performance and foveation; passthrough compositing on a phone; haptics (none are sent). The emulator draws both eyes into the one canvas, which is also what `bench/xr-*.png` show.

## Limits

- Experimental. Tested with an emulated device only.
- Imaging, the peel window, the post effects and order-independent ghosts are not drawn in a session (see above).
- No teleport, no hand-specific gestures beyond the `select` pinch, no depth sensing or occlusion of the model by the real room.
- Field AR: no tilt in the registration (gravity from the device is trusted), no floating mini-model for picking model points in the session, no camera pixels in snapshots (WebXR does not give them to a page), no persistent anchors on Android Chrome (re-tap 2 points), the in-scene panel of headsets shows text only (no buttons: the controller buttons act instead), and the cut is a box, not a sphere.
- iPhone and iPad: no WebXR in any browser; AR Quick Look shows a static export without registration.
- The model is not rescaled in AR (the viewer is), so very small physical sizes need a camera far plane that follows the rig scale (it does: `clipDistances`), and picking distances shown by the pointer are in model metres divided by the rig scale.
